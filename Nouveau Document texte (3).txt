  <h2>Most sleep advice is aimed at the wrong half of the night.</h2>

  <p>Almost everything you have been told — wind down earlier, cut the screens, keep the room cold — is about <em>getting to sleep.</em> And it works well enough. You get to sleep.</p>

  <p>Waking at three and staying awake is a different thing entirely. Sleep researchers call it sleep maintenance difficulty, and it is one of the most commonly reported sleep complaints among adults over forty. Knowing the name for it does not make the ceiling any more interesting at 3:14, but it does explain why the usual advice keeps missing.</p>

  <p class="pull">The goal is not falling asleep faster. It is staying under once you are there.</p>

  <p>That is the part most over-the-counter options handle badly. A lot of them are built to knock you down hard and fast, which is why people describe waking up feeling thick-headed and slow — and why plenty of people stop using them within a few weeks.</p>
